# Elevation and layers

## Elevation

Two shadow levels lift a surface off the page: `--v-shadow-raised` for small raised elements and
`--v-shadow-overlay` for floating panels such as menus. Both use one shared shadow color,
`--v-shadow-color`, which is much stronger in dark mode, where a faint shadow would disappear
against the background.

Both levels sit on `--v-color-surface-raised`. The two are told apart by their shadow, not by a
different surface color. In dark mode that surface is a step lighter than the page, so the lift
does not depend on the shadow alone.

A shadow is decoration. Do not rely on it to show where a control or container ends: give it a
`--v-color-border` as well, which is tested at 3:1
([1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)). Forced-colors modes
remove shadows entirely, and in light mode `surface-raised` is the same color as the page, so the
border is what is left to mark the edge.

## Layers

Stacking order comes from a short named scale, so components do not pick their own `z-index`
numbers. Each step sits above the one before it. Use the token that matches the kind of element,
and when something new needs to sit between two steps, discuss adding a step instead of using a
raw number.

To dim the page behind modal content, use `--v-color-surface-scrim`. It is documented with the
Color tokens.
