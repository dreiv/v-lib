# Color

Color tokens are named by **role**, not by hue. Reach for `--v-color-danger`, not for "red", and
the library decides what that means in light mode, in dark mode, and under the user's accent
color.

Every swatch on this page is rendered live with the real tokens. Light and dark examples sit in
their own `color-scheme` container, so both schemes are visible at once regardless of your system
setting.

## How color adapts

- **System colors.** `surface`, `text`, and `accent` follow the browser's own page background, text
  color, and accent color. The browser supplies the right value for the user's scheme and, where
  supported, their operating system accent.
- **Per-scheme values.** Status colors, `scrim`, and `accent-ink` are tuned separately for light and
  dark: deeper in light mode, lighter in dark mode, so they keep their contrast on either
  background. The tokens declare `color-scheme: light dark` on `:root`.
- **Blends.** Subtle surface, muted text, border, and divider are the text color blended into the
  surface at fixed strengths. Status surfaces blend a small share of the status color into the
  surface.
- **Reference tokens.** The `--v-ref-*` tokens hold the blend strengths the roles are built from.
  Components should consume the roles, not the reference tokens.

## Status colors in use

Status colors carry meaning, so never let color be the only signal: pair them with text or an
icon. Each status has a text color and a tinted surface, which is the status color blended into
the surface. The status color is tested at 4.5:1 on the plain surface, the subtle surface, and its
own tinted surface, and muted text is tested on all four tinted surfaces. Each row below shows one
status on those three backdrops.

## Accessibility

Color pairs are checked in `packages/tokens/tests/tokens.test.ts` by resolving each token to a
real color and computing the WCAG contrast ratio. Thresholds follow WCAG 2.2: 4.5:1 for text
([1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)) and 3:1 for
interface boundaries and focus indicators
([1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)).

The checks run against three backdrops: light (`#ffffff` background, `#000000` text), the dark
scheme as Chromium renders it (`#121212`), and the dark scheme as Firefox renders it (`#1c1b22`).
The contrast notes in the roles table and the pairs below come from one list.

### Accent colors

The accent comes from the operating system, so its value is unknown at build time. `accent-ink`
exists to make that safe: it blends the accent toward the text color, then limits its lightness so
it can always carry text. On each of the three backdrops above, and with each representative
system accent in the picker below, the tests assert:

- `accent-ink` on `surface` reaches 4.5:1, including under the hover and press overlays.
- `focus` on `surface` reaches 3:1.

Pick an accent to see `accent-ink` and the focus ring adapt.

### What the tests do not cover

- `divider` and `scrim` have no contrast assertion.
- `accent-text` on `accent` is decided by the operating system.
- Tests resolve colors with their own implementation of `color-mix()`, `light-dark()`, and
  relative color syntax, not in a browser. Review the live swatches above when you change a token.
